How to calculate work hours

  1. Add the date and start/end time for each shift.
  2. Enter only unpaid break minutes. Paid breaks should not be subtracted.
  3. Add more rows for additional shifts or split shifts.
  4. Review net hours, decimal hours and weekly totals.
  5. Download your timesheet as a CSV file for a spreadsheet.

Work hours examples

Day shift: 9:00 AM–5:30 PM with a 30-minute unpaid break = 8 hours (8.00 decimal hours).
Night shift: 10:00 PM–6:00 AM with a 45-minute unpaid break = 7 hours 15 minutes (7.25 decimal hours).
Part-time: 10:00 AM–2:30 PM with no break = 4 hours 30 minutes (4.50 decimal hours).

Frequently asked questions

How does the calculator handle overnight shifts?

If the end time is earlier than the start time, it is treated as the following day. For example, 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM is eight gross hours before unpaid breaks.

What is the difference between hours and decimal hours?

Hours and minutes show clock-style time, such as 7 hours 30 minutes. Decimal hours show the same duration as 7.5 hours. Thirty minutes is 0.5 of an hour, not 0.30.

Are breaks deducted automatically?

Only the unpaid break minutes you enter are deducted. Do not subtract paid breaks if they count as working time under your arrangement.

How is overtime calculated?

The calculator shows hours above the daily threshold you choose. It does not determine whether those hours legally qualify for overtime or calculate premiums, taxes or deductions. Check your contract and local rules.

How are weekly hours grouped?

Entries are grouped Monday through Sunday by the shift start date. An overnight shift is assigned to the date it starts in daily and weekly summaries.

Are my timesheet entries uploaded?

No. Calculations run in your browser. Entries are only stored in this browser if you enable Remember my entries. You can clear the saved copy at any time.

Can I use this as a payslip or official timesheet?

No. It is a time arithmetic helper and CSV export. Earnings, if entered, are a simple net-hours-times-rate estimate and exclude overtime premiums, taxes, deductions, rounding rules and employer policies.
