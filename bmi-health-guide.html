Start by understanding your result

Check the BMI value and the category shown by the calculator, but avoid treating the category as a diagnosis. Consider how you feel, any recent weight changes and other health information.

BMI does not directly measure body fat, muscle mass or fat distribution. A clinician can interpret it in context.

Build balanced nutrition habits

A varied eating pattern can include vegetables, fruit, whole grains, protein-rich foods and appropriate sources of healthy fats. Regular meals and sustainable portions are generally more useful than extreme restriction.

Avoid crash diets and unverified weight-loss products. Individual dietary needs vary, especially with medical conditions or medicines.

Choose movement you can maintain

Regular movement supports general health. Walking, cycling, swimming, household activity or other enjoyable options can help build consistency. Start at a comfortable level and increase gradually.

Adults are commonly advised to work towards 150–300 minutes of moderate activity each week and muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days, as health allows. Ask a clinician if you need an adapted plan.

Support sleep and daily routines

Consistent sleep and daily routines can support wellbeing. Break up long sitting periods, make time for rest and use realistic routines that fit your circumstances.

Sleep needs differ by age and individual. If sleep problems persist or affect daily life, consider professional advice.

Know when to seek professional guidance

Speak with a qualified healthcare professional if you have unexplained weight changes, persistent symptoms, concerns about eating or activity, or a medical condition that affects weight.

A clinician may consider waist measurement, blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol, medicines and medical history alongside BMI.
Health information notice: BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis. This guide provides general educational information and does not replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional.

? Frequently Asked Questions

Does BMI tell me whether I am healthy?

No. BMI is a screening measure and cannot assess overall health on its own.

What should I do after checking my BMI?

Use the result as one piece of information. Consider your overall habits and discuss concerns with a qualified healthcare professional.

Can I lose weight quickly based on my BMI result?

Avoid crash diets or extreme plans. If weight change is appropriate, seek evidence-based guidance tailored to your health.

How much activity should adults aim for?

A common public-health target is 150–300 minutes of moderate activity weekly, plus muscle-strengthening activity on two or more days, as health allows.

Should I use BMI during pregnancy?

Standard BMI categories are not intended to assess weight during pregnancy. Seek pregnancy-specific advice from your healthcare team.
